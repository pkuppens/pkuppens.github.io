Plan & Manage AI Solutions

The "before you build" domain: picking the right Foundry service for a task, standing up the infrastructure, then keeping it monitored, secured, and responsibly governed once it's live.

Choose the right Foundry service

Every AI-103 planning question reduces to: what kind of task is this, and which model/service class fits?

Task shapeChoose
Chat, reasoning, long-context generationA large language model (LLM)
Same task, cost/latency-sensitive, narrower scopeA small language model (SLM)
Input or output spans text + image/audio/videoA multimodal model
Prebuilt capability (speech, translation, document extraction) instead of a raw modelA Foundry Tool (Azure Speech, Translator, Content Understanding, …)
Answers must cite your own current dataRetrieval-augmented generation (RAG) + a vector/hybrid index
The system must plan, call tools, and remember context across turnsAn agent (Foundry Agent Service), with memory/tool/knowledge integration
Exam cue If the scenario says "just needs a quick answer, cost matters" → SLM. If it says "must reason over a photo and a question together" → multimodal model. If it says "must never make things up about our internal policy" → RAG. If it says "must take multi-step actions" → agent.

Set up AI solutions in Foundry

Planning infrastructure means answering four questions before you deploy anything:

QuestionWhat you decide
Where does compute for this app live?Azure infrastructure design for the AI app / agent-hosting tier
How is it deployed?Deployment option — standard vs. provisioned throughput, regional vs. global
How is the deployment itself configured?Model/agent deployment settings — capacity, content filters, versioning
How does it ship repeatably?Integrate the Foundry project with CI/CD so deployments aren't manual clicks

Manage, monitor, and secure AI systems

Once live, a Foundry solution needs the same operational discipline as any production system — plus AI-specific signals:

ConcernWhat you watch / configure
Cost & capacityQuotas, scaling, rate limits, cost footprint per model/agent workload
Model healthPerformance, drift, safety events, grounding quality
Data/search healthIngestion quality, search index health, relevance performance
SecurityManaged identity, private networking, keyless credentials, role-based access policies
Exam cue "Authenticate without embedding a key" → managed identity / keyless credentials. "Keep the model call off the public internet" → private networking. "Notice the model's answers slowly getting worse over time" → drift monitoring, not a one-time evaluation.

Implement responsible AI across generative and agentic systems

Responsible AI on AI-103 is operational, not philosophical — it's about the controls you configure and the evidence you keep:

ControlPurpose
Safety filters & guardrailsBlock or flag disallowed content before/after generation
Content moderationOngoing classification of generated/ingested content for risk
Evaluators & safety evaluationsInstrument the app to score fabrication, relevance, quality, and safety — not just eyeball outputs
Trace logging & provenance metadataAuditable record of what happened and where content came from
Approval workflowsA human checkpoint before a risky action executes
Oversight modes & tool-access controlsConstrain what an agent is allowed to do autonomously
