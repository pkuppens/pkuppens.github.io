Claude & AI Fluency Foundations

Three public Anthropic Academy courses, one domain: what Claude actually is, a working framework for collaborating with it well, and a mental model of why it sometimes gets things wrong. This is the vocabulary and judgment everything else in this course builds on.

Meet Claude (Claude 101)

Claude is Anthropic's family of models, used either conversationally (claude.ai, desktop/mobile apps) or built into products via the API. The public Claude 101 course structures learning Claude around four stages:

StageCovers
Meet ClaudeWhat Claude is, your first conversation, getting better results
Organizing work & knowledgeProjects (persistent context for a body of work), artifacts (standalone generated documents/code/UI you can iterate on), skills (packaged, reusable instructions)
Expanding Claude's reachConnecting your own tools, enterprise search, deep-dive research
Putting it togetherUse-cases by role
Vocabulary that matters later Projects and artifacts are Claude.ai concepts. Skills reappear, in a different but related form, inside Claude Code (Lesson 04) and as a platform feature (Lesson 03) — don't conflate the three; each is skills-the-same-idea applied to a different surface.

The 4D Framework (AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations)

Anthropic's AI Fluency course (built with academic partners) frames effective, ethical AI collaboration as four competencies, not a fixed prompt template:

CompetencyQuestion it answers
DelegationWhich parts of this task should go to the AI at all?
DescriptionHow do I state the task so the AI can actually do it?
DiscernmentIs this output actually good — and by what standard?
DiligenceWhose responsibility is the outcome, and did I verify it?

Description and Discernment aren't a one-shot pair — the course frames a Description–Discernment loop: you describe, evaluate the output, then re-describe to close the gap, repeating until the result clears your bar.

Why outputs surprise you (AI Capabilities and Limitations)

This course is the companion to the 4D framework: it teaches the machine side of Discernment — a mental model for why a model behaves as it does, built from four properties:

PropertyWhat it explains
Next-token predictionThe base mechanism — Claude generates one likely-next-token at a time, not by "looking up" a stored answer
KnowledgeWhat the model does and doesn't reliably know, and where that knowledge gets thin or stale
Working memoryWhat fits in the context window and how that bounds a single turn's reasoning
SteerabilityHow much your instructions can actually redirect behavior — and where that leverage runs out
The payoff Given an unexpected output, this framework lets you localize which property is the cause (a knowledge gap vs. a working-memory overflow vs. under-steering) and pick a targeted fix instead of just re-rolling the prompt.
